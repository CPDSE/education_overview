# Workflow for reviewing curriculum progression

The pilot examines progression across FA515 → FA503 → FA504. Connections identified in the teaching materials provide the starting point. Short reviews of specific exercises with the responsible teachers establish what students are actually required to do and how those expectations relate across courses.

## Purpose of the teacher reviews

The materials show possible connections, but classroom practice determines the demands placed on students. A guide does not establish whether students receive a completed script during class, whether an activity is required, or whether a decision must be explained in a report.

The reviews establish:

- What students must produce.
- What code, examples, hints, and assistance they receive.
- Which calculations and decisions they must make themselves.
- What earlier learning the activity assumes or explicitly recalls.

These questions support an account of curriculum progression. They do not require collecting student performance data.

## Workflow

1. **Prepare a concrete comparison.** Select linked activities from the [evidence mapping](../assets/data/evidence.csv). For example, FA503 supplies code for correcting sample removal, while FA504 asks students to write the corresponding calculation for Caco-2 transport.
2. **Check the interpretation with each teacher.** Show the relevant exercise and clarify the required output, available support, and decisions left to students. Record the teaching year and whether participation is required or optional. Use the [teacher review questions](review.md).
3. **Compare the confirmed expectations across courses.** Determine whether the connection represents increased independence, application in another context, consolidation, or different demands. Use the separate descriptors for independence, judgement, and transfer in the [pilot guide](README.md).
4. **Agree any changes needed.** Teachers might make an existing connection explicit, reduce repeated instructions, or introduce a missing opportunity to practise a capability. Record proposed changes separately from current practice.

## Example of a comparison

For the sampling correction in FA503 and FA504, resolve the following questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Do FA503 students work through and explain the supplied correction? | Establishes what the earlier course develops. |
| Do FA504 students construct the calculation before receiving a solution? | Establishes whether implementation independence increases. |
| Does FA504 explicitly expect students to draw on FA503? | Establishes whether the connection is intentional. |
| Must students explain why the correction is needed? | Distinguishes completing code from understanding the calculation. |

## Expected output

Produce a small number of agreed statements describing the relationship between specific activities. For example, if confirmed by the teachers:

> In FA503, students use and explain a supplied sampling correction. In FA504, they implement the same principle in a transport experiment, with equations and function hints provided.

This would document increased implementation independence and prompted transfer to another context. If both courses instead provide completed code, the finding would be repeated application with similar support.

For each statement, retain the evidence references, teacher confirmation, and any unresolved points in the [review record](review.md). The result gives CPDSE a reliable account of curriculum progression and gives teachers a basis for coordinating expectations between courses.
